Bill 16

Condo maintenance log: contents, review and responsibilities

November 12, 2026  ·  6-min read  ·  By Guillaume Prentki

The maintenance log is the foundation of Bill 16: it lists the building's components, estimates their useful life and schedules the work. The contingency fund study and much of the syndicate's certificate flow from it. Here is what it must contain, who can prepare it and what the board of directors must do next.

Key takeaways
  • The log is prepared by an engineer, chartered appraiser, architect or professional technologist whose work is mainly in real estate, with no connection to the co-ownership.
  • It schedules maintenance and replacement work over a 25-year horizon.
  • The board of directors must update it at least once a year and file it in the register within 60 days of receiving it.
  • A professional reviews it every 5 years, or every 10 years for some small buildings. Deadline for the first log: August 15, 2028.

What does a maintenance log contain?

The log has two sections.

1. Description of the components

  • the common portions;
  • the materials, devices and equipment in the common portions;
  • the materials, devices and equipment located in the private portions but maintained by the syndicate.

For each one: installation date, maintenance contracts, warranties, manufacturer's manual, and so on.

2. Condition, useful life and work to plan

The professional assesses the condition of each component and its remaining useful life, describes the work to be done and sets a schedule over 25 years, year by year. This schedule is then used to calculate contributions in the contingency fund study.

Who can prepare the maintenance log?

Only engineers, chartered appraisers, architects and professional technologists whose work is mainly in real estate management, construction, renovation or inspection. Chartered professional accountants can do the contingency fund study, but not the log.

A professional who is a co-owner, director, manager or occupant of the building cannot prepare its log, nor can one whose spouse or company holds one of these roles. A file kept by a director, however thorough, therefore does not replace the log required by law.

The syndicate's responsibilities

  • Hire an eligible professional for the log and have it reviewed at the required frequency.
  • Update it at least once a year: add any new information or document (work done, new warranties, inspection reports).
  • Justify work not done: the board must identify and explain any work required by the log that was not carried out.
  • Make it available: file it in the register and make it available to co-owners within 60 days of receiving it, or before the annual meeting if it falls within that period.

How often must it be reviewed?

On top of the board's annual update, a professional must review the log at least every 5 years. The review can be done every 10 years if the building meets one of these conditions:

  • it has no more than 8 private portions, not counting accessory portions such as storage and parking;
  • no common portion is located inside a building;
  • it has no more than 3 storeys entirely above ground.

Note: the contingency fund study, on the other hand, must be redone every 5 years for all co-ownerships.

Documents to gather before the mandate

The more information the professional finds, the fewer research hours they bill. Gather:

  • the declaration of co-ownership and the building plans;
  • invoices and reports for past work;
  • warranties, maintenance contracts and equipment manuals (heating, ventilation, elevators, roof, garage doors);
  • existing inspection and expert reports;
  • minutes that mention work or problems;
  • the history of claims and insurance claims.

These same documents will help you complete the syndicate's certificate, which must describe the inspections, claims and work of the last five years and the work planned for the next ten years.

Frequently asked questions

Is a maintenance log mandatory for a small condo?

Yes. All divided co-ownerships must have a maintenance log, regardless of size. Small buildings can only benefit from a review every 10 years instead of 5, if they meet one of the conditions provided.

Can a co-owner who is an engineer prepare the log for their own building?

No. Conflict-of-interest rules prevent a professional who is a co-owner, director, manager or occupant of the building, or whose spouse or company is, from preparing that building's log.

Who updates the maintenance log between reviews?

The board of directors, at least once a year. It adds new information and documents, and identifies and justifies any required work that was not done.

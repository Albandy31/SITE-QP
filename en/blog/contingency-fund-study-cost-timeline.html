Contingency fund study: cost, timelines and who can do it

November 12, 2026  ·  7-min read  ·  By Guillaume Prentki

The contingency fund study is the Bill 16 obligation that hits co-owners' wallets most directly: it sets how much the syndicate must put aside each year for major work. Here is who can do it, what it must contain, what it costs, how much time to allow, and what happens once you receive it.

Key takeaways
  • The study can be done by an engineer, chartered appraiser, architect, professional technologist (work mainly in real estate) or chartered professional accountant (CPA) with no connection to the co-ownership.
  • It is based on the maintenance log: the two documents are often ordered together.
  • Deadline for existing syndicates: August 15, 2028. After that, a new study is required at least every 5 years.
  • After the study, the board of directors sets the contribution within 30 days after the annual meeting; a shortfall can be made up over 10 years.

Who can do the contingency fund study?

The regulation limits the study to certain professionals:

  • engineers, chartered appraisers, architects and professional technologists, if their work is mainly in real estate management, construction, renovation or inspection;
  • chartered professional accountants (CPAs), who can do the study but not the maintenance log.

Conflict-of-interest rules apply: a professional who is a co-owner, director, manager or occupant of the building cannot prepare these documents, nor can one whose spouse or company holds one of these roles. Even if an engineer lives in the building, you must hire someone from outside.

What must the study contain?

At a minimum:

  • the contingency fund balance used for the study;
  • an estimate of the cost of each major repair and each replacement, in the year set out in the maintenance log;
  • a recommendation on the minimum amount the fund should hold at the start of each year and on the amounts to pay into it each year;
  • where applicable, the portion reserved for restricted common portions (balconies, parking, etc.);
  • an explanation of the calculations.

The study is based on the maintenance log, which sets a schedule of work over 25 years. Without an up-to-date log, there is no reliable study: that is why both mandates are often given to the same professional.

How much does a contingency fund study cost?

The price depends mainly on the size of the building, the number of components to analyze, the state of the existing documentation and whether the maintenance log is included. As a guide, the ranges commonly seen are:

  • Small building (6 to 20 units): about $3,000 to $6,000;
  • Mid-size building (21 to 60 units): about $6,000 to $12,000;
  • Large building (over 60 units): $12,000 to $25,000 and more.

These ranges do not replace a quote: ask for at least three comparable ones, specifying whether the maintenance log is included. Well-organized documentation (plans, invoices, warranties, inspection reports) reduces the time billed.

Good to know

The study is an expense of the syndicate. Compared with an unexpected special assessment of several thousand dollars per unit when the roof or windows need replacing, it is an investment.

What timelines should you plan for?

Existing syndicates have until August 15, 2028. In practice, you need time to choose the professional, gather the documents, inspect the building, produce the log and then the study, and correct the preliminary report. Since every syndicate in Quebec has the same deadline and eligible professionals are in high demand, it is better to request quotes well before 2028.

A study obtained after August 14, 2023 from an eligible, independent professional is recognized and remains valid for five years. An older study must be redone.

What happens after the study?

  1. Annual meeting: the board of directors presents, for consultation, the contribution required by the study, adjusted for changes in the building (work done or postponed).
  2. Board decision: within 30 days after the meeting, the board sets the contribution, which co-owners then start paying.
  3. Catch-up: if the fund is underfunded, the catch-up can be spread over 10 years.
  4. Update: a new study is required at least every 5 years, for all co-ownerships.

Until the first study, the syndicate must pay into the fund at least 5% of the contributions to common expenses (transition meeting before July 15, 2025) or 0.5% of the reconstruction value (after that date).

Frequently asked questions

Can an accountant do the contingency fund study?

Yes. In addition to eligible engineers, chartered appraisers, architects and professional technologists, chartered professional accountants (CPAs) can do the study. They cannot, however, prepare the maintenance log.

How much does a contingency fund study cost in Quebec?

It depends mainly on the size of the building. Typical ranges go from about $3,000 to $6,000 for 6 to 20 units, up to $12,000 and more above 60 units. Ask for at least three comparable quotes.

How often must the study be redone?

At least every 5 years, for all co-ownerships. The maintenance log, on the other hand, can be reviewed every 10 years for some small buildings.

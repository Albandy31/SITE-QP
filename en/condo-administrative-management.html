Pillar 1 · Administrative

Administrative and legal condo management.

Meetings, board of directors, registers, insurance and legal obligations: we keep your condo corporation's structure up to date, rigorously.

Request my free quote →

Updated: November 12, 2026

A condo corporation is a legal entity with specific obligations: keep its registers, call and hold its meetings, insure the building, file its returns and comply with Bill 16. When these tasks fall on volunteer board members, they slip, and it is often during a sale or a claim that people find out. Our administrative pillar takes all of this over.

What our administrative management includes

General meetings

We prepare the agenda with the board of directors, send the notices of meeting on time and attend the annual general meeting. A well-prepared meeting, with clear documents sent in advance, runs better and its decisions hold. Special meetings can also be organized when needed.

Board of directors

We call, run and attend board meetings, twice a year in the Serenity plan. The board keeps the decisions; we prepare the files, follow up and carry them out.

Registers and declaration of co-ownership

We keep the declaration of co-ownership and the register of co-owners up to date, as the law requires. A complete, centralized register is also what makes it possible to provide the corporation's certificate within 15 days when a unit is sold.

Insurance

We manage the building's insurance contract and its renewal. Up-to-date coverage, based on the reconstruction value, protects the corporation and its directors.

Legal filings and Bill 16

We prepare the annual filings with the Registraire des entreprises (REQ) and continuously track Bill 16 obligations: maintenance log, contingency fund study and the corporation's certificate, with a deadline of August 15, 2028 for existing corporations.

Corporation employees

If your corporation hires staff, such as a janitor, we handle their hiring and payroll.

Why it matters

  • Valid decisions: a meeting called incorrectly can be challenged, and its decisions with it.
  • Sales without delays: without an up-to-date register and documents, the corporation's certificate becomes a headache when an owner sells.
  • Protected directors: adequate insurance and legal obligations met reduce the risks for volunteer board members.

A leak or a major failure cannot wait until Monday morning. The faster the response, the smaller the damage, the costs and the insurance claims. That is why every condo corporation we manage has access to an emergency line open at all times.

What counts as an emergency?

Call the emergency line for any incident that affects the common areas or several units and cannot wait until the next business day:

  • Water damage: active leak, sewer backup, burst pipe, water coming in through the roof or windows.
  • Elevator breakdown, especially if someone is trapped (also call 911) or residents with reduced mobility are affected.
  • Major mechanical failure: no heat in winter, power outage in the common areas, central water heater, pump or alarm system.
  • Building security: main or garage door that no longer locks, broken window, break-in.
  • Any incident that threatens occupants' safety or could quickly make the damage worse.

Non-urgent requests (noise, parking, small repairs, questions about fees) are handled during business hours, by email or phone.

What we do when you call

  1. Assess the situation with you: what is happening, where, and whether there is any danger.
  2. Limit the damage: we guide you to shut off the water or power if it is safe, and we dispatch the right contractor (plumber, electrician, elevator technician, locksmith).
  3. Inform the people affected: the owners and occupants of the units concerned.
  4. Document the incident: photos, contractor reports and a timeline, useful for the corporation's insurance.
  5. Follow up with the board of directors: repairs, insurance claim and steps to prevent it from happening again.

Water damage: what to do

Water damage is the most common emergency in a condominium. While waiting for the contractor:

  • Close the water valve of the appliance or unit if you know where it is.
  • Cut the power in the wet area only if you can do so safely.
  • Warn your neighbours above and below.
  • Take photos and videos before cleaning up, and do not throw away damaged items before the insurer agrees.
  • Notify your own insurer: the corporation's policy and the owner's policy often both apply.

Who pays for what depends on the declaration of co-ownership, the source of the damage and the insurance policies. To avoid common mistakes, read our article on the 7 pitfalls of condo insurance claims.

Prevention beats repairs

Many emergencies give warning signs: a slow drain, a stain on the ceiling, an elevator that stops unevenly. An up-to-date maintenance log, regular inspections and well-managed maintenance contracts mean fewer calls in the middle of the night. Since Bill 16, a maintenance log is mandatory for every condo corporation: see our Bill 16 guide.

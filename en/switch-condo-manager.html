Switch condo managers

Switch condo managers, without the stress.

Your current manager no longer responds, financial statements are late or Bill 16 is not moving forward? Here are the 7 steps to change managers while protecting your corporation.

Updated: November 11, 2026

Changing condo managers often worries board members: fear of losing documents, of blocking payments or of confusing the owners. Well prepared, however, the transition happens without any interruption of service. Here is how to proceed.

Signs it is time to change

  • Emails and calls go unanswered for days.
  • Financial statements or bank reconciliations are late, or never arrive.
  • Suppliers are paid late, or overdue fees are not followed up.
  • The maintenance log and contingency fund study required by Bill 16 are not moving forward.
  • General meetings are poorly prepared and minutes are late.
  • Owners complain directly to board members because they have no one else to talk to.

The 7 steps to switch condo managers

1. Review the current management contract

Find the contract term, its end date, the required termination notice and any penalties. Some contracts renew automatically if notice is not sent in time: write down the deadline.

2. Define your needs and request quotes

List what is not working and what you expect: included services, response times, financial reports, emergency handling, Bill 16 follow-up. Then request quotes from two or three managers. Our guide 7 criteria to choose a condo manager will help you compare them.

3. Have the board of directors adopt the decision

Choosing the manager is generally the board's responsibility. Record the decision in a resolution in the minutes: end of the current contract, choice of the new manager and transition date. Also check what your declaration of co-ownership provides.

4. Send the termination notice

Send written notice to your current manager, respecting the notice period in the contract. State the end date of the mandate and ask for all of the corporation's documents to be handed over on that date.

5. Get all of the corporation's records back

The registers and documents belong to the corporation. Make a list and check each item at handover:

  • declaration of co-ownership, by-laws and register of co-owners;
  • minutes of general meetings and board meetings;
  • financial statements, budgets, general ledger, supporting documents and bank statements;
  • list of overdue fees by owner;
  • service contracts, warranties, insurance policies and claims history;
  • maintenance log, contingency fund study, plans and inspection reports;
  • keys, access cards, codes and access to online platforms.

6. Take over the accounting and banking

The new manager reconciles the accounts at the transition date, has the bank signatories updated according to the board's resolution and makes sure fee collection and supplier payments continue without interruption.

7. Inform the owners and launch the new mandate

Owners receive a clear message: the new manager's name, contact details, emergency line and any changes to how fees are paid. A kick-off meeting with the board then sets the priorities for the first months.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about switching condo managers.

How long does it take to switch condo managers?+
The transition generally takes 15 to 30 days. It includes getting the records back, the accounting takeover, updating the register of co-owners and communicating with residents.
Can we switch managers before the contract ends?+
It depends on your current contract. Review the termination clause: it sets out the notice period and any penalties. If in doubt, have it checked before sending the notice.
Who decides to change condo managers?+
Generally the board of directors, through a resolution recorded in the minutes. Also check what your declaration of co-ownership provides.
What if the former manager is slow to hand over the documents?+
The registers and documents belong to the corporation. A written request setting a specific deadline is usually enough. If it has no effect, the board can consult a lawyer.
